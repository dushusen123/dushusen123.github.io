Screen Print vs DTG vs Embroidery: Which to Choose?

The three main decoration methods for custom t-shirts aren't interchangeable. Each has a cost floor, a durability ceiling and a quantity sweet spot. Here's how to pick with the numbers.

Screen Printing: The Workhorse

Screen printing pushes ink through a mesh stencil. It's the cheapest, most durable method at volume — and the default for any brand ordering 100+ pieces.

Why it's cheap at volume: Setup is $20–35 per color. At 50 pieces, that's $0.40–0.70 per shirt just in setup. At 500 pieces, it's $0.04–0.07. That's why per-unit screen print cost drops from ~$5 at 50 pcs to under $1 at 500 pcs.

Why it's durable: Plastisol and water-based inks sit on the fabric surface and bond after curing. A properly screen-printed logo survives 50+ washes without cracking. ["https://www.rallytowels.com/custom-t-shirts-screen-printing-vs-dtg-vs-embroidery-vs-dye-sublimation-2026/"]

When to use it: Simple 1–6 color designs, bold graphics, 50+ pieces. Not ideal for photorealistic art or gradients.

DTG: The Small-Run Option

Direct-to-Garment prints like an inkjet printer directly onto fabric. No screens, no setup, unlimited colors.

Why it's better at low volume: Zero setup fee means the first shirt costs the same as the 50th. At 10 pieces, DTG is $6.50/unit vs screen print's $8+/unit (because screen setup hasn't been spread out yet). The crossover point where screen print becomes cheaper is around 20–50 pieces. ["https://iehk.com/how-to-choose-a-garment-printing-method/"]

The trade-off: DTG ink is thinner, so it fades faster on dark fabrics and isn't as durable as screen print after 30+ washes. It also requires pre-treatment on dark tees, which adds cost and can leave a faint box around the print.

When to use it: Test orders under 50 pieces, full-color/photographic designs, one-off event merch.

Embroidery: The Premium Signal

Embroidery stitches thread directly into the fabric. It's the most expensive method but it's the only one that genuinely makes a garment feel "premium."

The cost driver is stitch count. A small left-chest logo (2.5" wide, ~5,000 stitches) adds $2–4 per garment at volume. A large back logo (30,000+ stitches) can add $8–15. Digitizing (converting your logo file to stitch data) is a one-time $25–60 fee. ["https://apparel.alibaba.com/guide/custom-printing-guide-for-apparel-wearables"]

Why brands pay for it: Embroidery doesn't crack, fade or peel. It looks upscale on polos, caps, hoodies and workwear. Customers perceive embroidered garments as worth more — even if the blank is identical.

When to use it: Small logos (under 4"), premium positioning, polos/hoodies/caps. Avoid on very thin or stretchy fabrics.

What Most Brands Actually Do

They combine methods. A typical premium program uses embroidery on the left chest (brand mark, durable, premium feel) and screen print on the back or sleeve (graphic, cheap at volume). You don't have to pick one — just ask your factory to quote both.
