How to Import Clothing from China to the USA

FOB price is only half the number. Here's the full landed-cost math — duty rates, Section 301 tariffs, freight options and the HTS codes that actually apply to your garments.

Step 1: Find Your HTS Code

Every apparel item has a 10-digit US Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) code. Getting it wrong means paying the wrong duty — or a customs penalty. The two most common for knits:

GarmentHTS CodeBase duty (MFN)
Cotton knit t-shirts6109.10.0016.5%
Man-made fiber knit t-shirts6109.90.0032.0%
Cotton knit sweaters/hoodies6110.20~14.9%
Cotton woven shirts6205.20~19.7%

Sources: USITC HTS Database; Dantful Logistics 2025; Commenda HS Codes Guide 2026. ["https://www.htshub.com/us-hs/search/6109","https://dantful.us/shipping-clothing-from-china-to-usa/"]

The first six digits are global; the US adds four more. Look up your exact code at hts.usitc.gov — don't guess.

Step 2: Add the China Tariffs

China-origin goods face an additional Section 301 tariff on top of the MFN rate. For most apparel the Section 301 rate is currently 7.5% (some categories are 25%, depending on the list). ["https://www.greenwich-mercantile.com/es/resources/guides/import-duties-clothing-consumer-goods"]

Worked example — 1,000 cotton t-shirts, FOB $5.00 each:

Line itemCalculationAmount
FOB value1,000 × $5.00$5,000.00
MFN duty (16.5%)$5,000 × 0.165$825.00
Section 301 (7.5%)$5,000 × 0.075$375.00
MPF (merchandise processing fee ~0.3464%)$5,000 × 0.003464$17.32
Total duty + fees$1,217.32
Landed duty per shirt$1,217.32 / 1,000$1.22

Methodology per Eton Garment US Duty Calculator 2026. ["https://etongarment.com/faq/how-much-will-customs-duties-be-for-importing-apparel-from-china-to-the-us/"]

So a $5 FOB tee actually lands in the US at about $6.22 — before freight. Budget ~24–26% of FOB value for duty and fees on cotton knits.

For a 500-pc test order, sea LCL to Los Angeles typically runs $400–700 all-in (delivery to your door). That's roughly $1–1.40 per shirt in freight — far cheaper than air.

Step 4: Documents You Need

  • Commercial invoice — from the factory, with HTS code, country of origin, unit price, total value.
  • Packing list — carton count, dimensions, gross weight.
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) — from the forwarder once the container sails.
  • Customs bond — required for formal entries (shipments over $2,500). A one-time $50–100 annual bond is standard.
  • IOR (importer of record) — you, with a US tax ID (EIN or SSN).

Shipments under $2,500 can enter as "informal entries" without a bond — but for a real brand program, budget for formal entry and a customs broker ($100–200 per shipment).

Common New-Brand Mistakes

"I'll just use Alibaba shipping." It's convenient, but the price is usually 20–40% higher than what you'll get from a forwarder once you know your volume. Use it for the first order, then switch.

"Under-declare the value to save duty." Customs audits are real, and under-valuation penalties hit the importer — not the factory. Declare the real FOB value.

"I'll figure out the HTS code when it arrives." Customs holds shipments with wrong or missing codes. Do it before the goods leave China.
