Understanding Fabric Weight: 180gsm vs 240gsm vs 300gsm

GSM is the single most important number on a custom t-shirt spec sheet. It changes hand feel, durability, print quality and price. Here's what each range actually feels like — and which to pick for your brand.

What Is GSM?

GSM = Grams per Square Meter. It measures fabric density: how much a one-square-meter piece of material weighs. Higher GSM means more yarn, more structure, usually more durability. It is not the same as thread count, and it doesn't tell you everything — but it's the first number every buyer should specify.

What Changes When You Go Heavier?

Price. Fabric is 45–55% of FOB cost. Moving from 180gsm to 240gsm on the same cotton typically adds 20–30% to fabric cost, and therefore about 10–15% to the finished FOB price.

Opacity. 180gsm white tees show a tank top underneath. 240gsm doesn't. This matters a lot for women's tees and light colors.

Print quality. Heavier fabric holds ink better. DTG on 180gsm can show print-through; on 240gsm the print looks cleaner.

Shape retention. After 10 washes, a 180gsm crew neck will likely have a relaxed, slightly floppy collar. A 240gsm will still look like the day it arrived.

Shrinkage. Pre-shrunk 200gsm typically shrinks under 3%. The lower the GSM, the more shrinkage variance you'll see — budget for it in size grading.

Which GSM Should Your Brand Use?

Your brandRecommended GSM
Budget basics / fast fashion160–180 gsm
Everyday casual, balanced quality180–200 gsm
Streetwear / premium basics220–240 gsm
Luxury / heavyweight tees260–300 gsm
Summer-specific / tropical markets160–180 gsm (don't overdo it)

A Common Mistake: Equating GSM With Quality

A 240gsm shirt made from cheap, carded cotton will feel rougher than a 180gsm shirt made from combed or ring-spun cotton. GSM measures weight, not hand feel. Specify both:

  • Fiber: 100% cotton (organic or conventional), cotton/poly blend, tri-blend.
  • Yarn count: Combed, carded, ring-spun, compact.
  • Weight: e.g. 200 gsm.
  • Knitting: Single jersey, interlock, French terry, fleece.

"200 gsm combed ring-spun cotton single jersey" is a spec. "A good heavy t-shirt" is a conversation. Give the factory the full spec — otherwise they'll pick whatever's cheapest that roughly matches your words.
